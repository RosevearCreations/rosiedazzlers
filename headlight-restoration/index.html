How restoration works

  1. Inspect both lenses for surface oxidation, pitting, cracks, internal haze/moisture and assembly damage.
  2. Clean the lenses and mask surrounding paint and trim.
  3. Choose the least aggressive abrasive progression that safely removes the failed surface layer.
  4. Refine and machine-polish the surface back toward useful clarity.
  5. Clean the corrected lens and apply the appropriate UV-protection stage.
  6. Reinspect the pair and explain the improvement, remaining defects and aftercare.

What restoration cannot fix

  • deep cracks or structural lens failure
  • internal condensation or haze inside the sealed assembly
  • reflector or electrical damage
  • severe crazing or deterioration through the material
  • damage that requires a replacement lens or complete headlight assembly

We aim for the best safe improvement; we do not promise a new-lens result when the damage is not limited to the exterior surface.

Before we arrive

  • Send clear photos from the front and an angle if the lens is heavily yellowed, rough or cracked.
  • Tell us if the headlights have been restored, sanded or coated before.
  • Provide a safe/private work area around the front corners. Rosie brings standard detailing water and power; unusual access, parking or local runoff restrictions should be noted before dispatch.

Aftercare

  • Avoid harsh solvents and abrasive automatic-brush cleaning on the restored lens.
  • Wash gently and follow any cure instructions given for the protection product used.
  • Inspect the lenses over time and renew appropriate UV protection as needed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know which price tier applies?

Photos can establish a likely tier, but hands-on inspection confirms oxidation depth, coating failure and whether any damage is internal rather than surface-level.

Will the headlights look brand new?

Not every lens can return to new condition. Surface oxidation can often improve substantially; cracks, internal moisture, reflector damage and severe crazing remain limitations.

Why not just polish the yellow layer?

A quick polish may improve appearance temporarily, but deeper failed material can require sanding/refining, and the corrected lens still needs appropriate protection from renewed UV exposure.

Can I send photos before booking?

Yes. Use the photo-estimate path for severe or uncertain condition so we can steer you toward the right tier before the appointment.
